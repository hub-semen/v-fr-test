Asian Handicap Betting Explained

Aug 5, 2026 · 6 min read · By the VOdds Team

Asian Handicap removes the draw from a match by giving one side a head start, expressed as a fraction of a goal. It is the default market at most Asian sportsbooks because it forces a cleaner, lower-margin price than a standard 1x2 line.

What the handicap does

A handicap of -1 on the favourite means that team must win by two goals or more for the bet to pay out; win by exactly one and the bet is a push (stake returned); lose or draw and the bet loses. The underdog, at +1, wins the bet unless they lose by two or more.

Whole, half and quarter lines

  • Whole handicaps (-1, -2) can result in a push, where the stake is simply returned.
  • Half handicaps (-0.5, -1.5) never push — there is always a winner, since no match finishes on a half-goal margin.
  • Quarter handicaps (-0.25, -0.75) split the stake between the two adjacent whole or half lines, so part of the bet can win while the rest pushes or loses.

Reading the payout

A -0.25 handicap on a favourite is really two equal half-stakes: one at 0 (draw no bet) and one at -0.5. If the favourite wins, both halves win. If the match draws, the 0 half pushes and the -0.5 half loses, so you get back half your stake plus winnings on the other half. If the favourite loses, both halves lose.

Why books favor this market

Removing the draw as a distinct outcome lets a book price much closer to true 50/50 odds on either side of the handicap, which means a tighter margin for the bettor compared to a three-way 1x2 market. That is the main reason Asian Handicap and totals dominate at books like PS3838 and SingBet.
